# Module 4: Equipment, Gas Blending & Oxygen Analysis

## 1. The 40% Oxygen Boundary (Equipment Rules)

Pure or high-percentage oxygen accelerates combustion — oil, grease, silicone, or adiabatic compression heat can ignite inside valves/regulators.

| Oxygen % | Standard Equipment Rule |
|---|---|
| **22% – 40% $O_2$ (Recreational Nitrox)** | Outside the EU (including Australia), **standard recreational equipment** is commonly used with mixes up to **40% $O_2$** ("Oxygen Clean" cylinder required if **partial-pressure blending** with 100% $O_2$!). *(Note: European Community norms require "Oxygen Service" for equipment used above **22% $O_2$** — RAID exams sometimes test this regional distinction.)* |
| **> 40% $O_2$ (Decompression / Tech)** | **100% oxygen-service dedicated & cleaned** cylinders, valves, O-rings, lubricants, and regulators are strictly mandatory everywhere. |

- **Non-compatible materials (RAID Exam List):**
  - **Buna-N (neoprene) O-rings** and **neoprene seats/diaphragms** (incidental exposure only — use **Viton** or **Ethylene-Propylene / EPDM** instead).
  - **Silicone grease** (e.g., Dow-Corning 111 is unacceptable for high-pressure EANx/O2 — use fluorocarbon greases like **Fluorolube, Halocarbon, 3M Kel-Fm, or Fomblin**; never petroleum lubricants).
  - **Non-oxygen-rated** high/low pressure hoses.
- **Cylinder identification:** Nitrox tanks must be yellow with a `10 cm` green band or have a green/yellow **"ENRICHED AIR NITROX (22% to 40%)"** decal and current visual/hydro inspection.
- **Keep air tanks & Nitrox tanks separate:** Never top off a partial-pressure O2-clean Nitrox tank with an unfiltered/oil-lubricated air compressor.

## 2. How Nitrox is Blended (Overview)

You don't need to be a gas blender to get Nitrox certified, but exams test the 3 blending methods:
1. **Partial-Pressure Blending:** Pure $O_2$ is added to an O2-clean cylinder first, then topped up with oil-free ("oxygen-compatible") air. *(Highest fire risk during filling — requires O2-clean tank).*
2. **Continuous / Membrane / Nitrox Stick Blending:** Air is mixed with $O_2$ (or stripped of $N_2$ via a permeable membrane) *before* compression, so the cylinder never sees $>40\%\ O_2$.
3. **Premix / Storage Bank:** Large bank cylinders hold pre-blended EAN32/EAN36.

## 3. Analyzing Your Own Tank (The Non-Negotiable Practical Skill)

> **Golden Rule of Nitrox Diving:** **Every diver MUST personally analyze their own cylinder before diving it** — never trust someone else's word, tag, or shop fill sheet alone.

### Step-by-Step Oxygen Analyzer Procedure (What You Will Demonstrate in the Shop)

1. **Inspect tank markings:** Check hydro/visual dates and Nitrox green/yellow decal.
2. **Turn on and warm up the oxygen analyzer** (electrochemical **galvanic fuel cell** containing **potassium hydroxide electrolyte**, kept at the same room temperature as the gas).
3. **Calibrate with known gas (usually dry air = `20.9%` or `21%`):**
   - Expose sensor to dry ambient air away from breath/wind, adjust to `20.9%` (RAID also notes verifying `98–99%` on pure O2 if calibrating full range, then flushing back to `20.9%` in air).
4. **Flow Nitrox gas across the sensor gently:**
   - Crack the cylinder valve slightly (or use a flow restrictor/adaptor) — **slow, steady flow (~1–2 L/min)**. High pressure/flow cools or damages the sensor and gives false high readings!
   - Hold sensor steadily until reading stabilizes (~10–20 seconds).
   - Acceptable tolerance: within **$\pm 1\%$** of your planned/requested mix (e.g. target `32%` reads `31.0%–33.0%`).
5. **Re-check calibration** if readings drift or look unexpected (low battery or expired KOH fuel cell causes erratic/lagging readings).
6. **Label and Sign the Cylinder & Fill Log:**
   - Use a **permanent marker** (**NEVER a grease pencil**, which smudges — RAID Academic p. 38) on the contents-ID label/tape: **Actual analyzed $FO_2\%$**, **MOD** (and TOD), **Fill Date**, and **Diver's Name/Initials** (also log fill pressure, serial number, cert, and signature in the shop Nitrox logbook).
   - **Mark MOD and your name prominently** on the tank so you and your buddy see it underwater.

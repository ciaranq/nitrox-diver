I do have some notes though:

At 16:50 your slide defines Dalton's and Henry's law in the same way, even though they're different. 

Dalton's law states that in a mixture of gases, the total pressure is equal to the sum total of each gases' partial pressure. 

You then made a statement where I believe you mispoke, since you mixed partial pressures with percentages. Indeed, even though the partial pressure of the gases change as we dive, that's not the same as saying that the percentages of each gass also changes. For example, at sea level, a tank of air will have 21% oxygen and 79% nitrogen, with 0.21 and 0.79 partial pressures respectively. At a depth of 10 meters (2 atmospheres), their percentages in the gas will still be 21% and 79%, but their partial pressures will change in accordance with Dalton's law (so, for example, Oxygen will have a partial pressure of 0.42 and nitrogen of 1.58).

## 1. Core Nitrox Principles & Gas Laws
- **Definition & Levels:** Nitrox (EANx) is any $N_2/O_2$ mix over `21%` $O_2$. SSI Level 1 covers `32%`, Level 2 covers `36%`, up to `40%` maximum recreational $O_2$.
- **Benefits vs Tradeoffs:** Longer NDLs, shorter surface intervals, and extra safety margin if diving air limits on Nitrox (though the instructor recommends setting your dive computer to the actual analyzed $O_2\%$ rather than diving it on Air mode so you track true limits). Does **not** prevent DCS or let you dive deeper.
- **Henry's Law ("Kool-Aid Law"):** Gas dissolves into blood/tissues in proportion to partial pressure, agitation (heart rate/exertion), and temperature (colder water dissolves more gas).
- **Dalton's Law:** Total pressure equals the sum of partial pressures ($P_{\text{total}} = PpO_2 + PpN_2$).
  - *Video erratum noted in comments (`16:50`):* The instructor loosely said "42% oxygen / 158% nitrogen" at `10 m` (`2 bar`) — the **percentages** in your tank never change (`21%` / `79%`), only the **partial pressures** double to **`0.42 bar`** and **`1.58 bar`**.

## 2. Hypoxia vs Hyperoxia & Best Mix / MOD Math
- **Hypoxia thresholds ($PpO_2$):**
  - `0.16–0.17 bar`: Hypoxic symptoms (impaired coordination/acuity).
  - `0.12 bar`: Minimum to remain conscious.
  - `0.10 bar`: Blackout / fatal threshold.
- **Hyperoxia / Recreational Limits ($PpO_2$):**
  - `1.1 bar`: Lower threshold where oxygen toxicity risk starts over long exposure.
  - **`1.4 bar`**: Recommended maximum **working** $PpO_2$.
  - **`1.6 bar`**: Absolute maximum $PpO_2$ ceiling.
- **Formula traps highlighted:** Always watch order of operations:
  - $\text{Best Mix} = \dfrac{1.4}{(D / 10) + 1}$ *(divide `1.4` by ambient pressure — not the reverse!)*.
  - $\text{MOD (m)} = \left(\dfrac{1.4}{FO_2} - 1\right) \times 10$.

## 3. Oxygen Toxicity, DCS & First-Aid Exam Questions
- **Pulmonary $O_2$ Toxicity (`0.45–1.6 bar` over hours):** Throat irritation $\rightarrow$ dry/uncontrollable cough $\rightarrow$ chest tightness; mild cases heal in 2–4 weeks. Distinct from **Immersion Pulmonary Edema (IPE)**, where fluid fills the lungs and a diver feels out of breath despite a full tank.
- **CNS $O_2$ Toxicity (`>1.4–1.6 bar`):** **CONVENTID** (**Con**vulsions, **V**isual changes, **E**ars ringing, **N**ausea, **T**witching [face], **I**rritability, **D**izziness). Know which signs a buddy can see (facial twitching, convulsions) vs what only you feel (tinnitus, vision changes, dizziness).
- **Exam Q&As walked through in class (~42:30–48:00):**
  - **Cyanosis (bluish skin)** indicates hypoxia or hypothermia — **not** typical DCS (whereas weakness, extreme fatigue, and tingling/numbness are DCS symptoms).
  - **Suspected DCS in a remote location:** Give 100% $O_2$, monitor vitals, hydrate, transport — **never recompress the diver underwater**.
  - **Contaminated tank air:** Caused by bad compressor intake placement, poor maintenance, or oil vapours (all of the above).

## 4. Decompression Theory, Safety Stops & Dive Computers
- **Models:** Haldane (2:1 ratio) $\rightarrow$ Workman (**M-value**: max tissue gas load before supersaturation/bubbling on ascent) $\rightarrow$ Bühlmann **`ZH-L16C`** (16 fast-to-slow tissue compartments).
- **Reducing DCS risk:** Stay hydrated, stay warm (reduces DCS risk ~3–4x), light aerobic exercise ~19 hours pre-dive, and always do a **5-minute safety stop** at `5 m` (or `6 m` for 1 min + `3 m` for 4 min — transcript cites a bubble-study drop to zero detectable bubbles by 42 min vs 119,000 bubbles/L with no stop).
- **Dive computers are NOT biometric:** They track multi-level profiles accurately, but cannot measure your sleep, hydration, cold stress, or day-to-day physiology.

## 5. Practical Tank Analysis & Marking (Last 10 Minutes)
- **Never skip analyzing your own tank:** Even if a shop worker insists they already analyzed it, personally analyze every tank you breathe.
- **Analyzer technique:** Calibrate on known air (`21%`), then crack the valve **gently** (soft "ssss", never blast) until the reading stabilizes.
- **Rule of 40 & O2 cleaning:** Standard recreational scuba gear handles up to `40%` $O_2$; tanks used for pure $O_2$ blending must be O2-cleaned to remove hydrocarbons (fire/explosion hazard).
- **Practical tank marking tip:** Skip paper tags that disintegrate — write **$O_2\%$, MOD in metres (`m`), date, and your name/signature** in permanent marker on duct tape, and stick a large **MOD in metres** label on the back/bottom of the cylinder so your buddy behind you can immediately flag if you drift too deep.
